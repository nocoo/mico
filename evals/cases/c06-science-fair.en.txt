Thank you for coming to the science fair planning meeting. We have about forty minutes and we need to leave with a budget, a schedule, a venue plan, and named owners. The fair is for grades six through eight, it will run on a Saturday in October, and we expect one hundred and twenty student projects. Last year we had eighty projects and it was already crowded, so this year we need a different layout.

Let's start with the goal. The fair should be a low pressure opportunity for students to explain their work, not a pure competition. Every student gets a table and a short presentation slot. We will have three judged categories: scientific method, engineering design, and communication. We also want a family open house in the afternoon so parents and younger students can visit.

The venue options are the school gym, the cafeteria plus hallway, and the community college atrium. The gym is free and can hold one hundred and twenty tables, but the acoustics are terrible and the floor is needed for basketball on Friday night, so setup cannot start until seven in the morning. The cafeteria and hallway can hold ninety tables and setup can start Friday afternoon, but the hallway is a fire lane and we cannot block it. The college atrium is beautiful and has parking, but it costs eight hundred dollars and requires a certificate of insurance.

The safety requirements are not optional. Every project using heat, chemicals, or live animals needs a pre approval form signed by a teacher. No open flames. Electrical projects must use grounded outlets and cannot daisy chain power strips. We need two first aid stations and a clear path for emergency vehicles. The district safety officer has to review the floor plan two weeks before the event.

The budget we have is four thousand dollars from the PTA, plus whatever we raise. Last year we spent two thousand on tables, six hundred on printing, four hundred on awards, and three hundred on snacks. This year we want to add a keynote speaker and a livestream for families who cannot attend. The speaker honorarium is five hundred, the AV rental is seven hundred, and the livestream requires a volunteer and a dedicated laptop.

Let's compare the two realistic venue plans. Plan A is the gym plus the outdoor courtyard for food trucks. Capacity is good, setup is painful, and the acoustics are poor. Plan B is the cafeteria, the hallway, and four classrooms for quiet presentations. Capacity is ninety projects, so we would need to limit entries or run two sessions. The college atrium is a third option but the insurance and cost make it a stretch.

The committee's preference is Plan A if we can solve three problems: setup time, noise, and power. The custodial team says they can open the gym at six, not seven, if we submit a request two weeks ahead. The AV club can lend us two portable speakers and six microphones. The facilities team can bring in two quiet generators for the courtyard, but we need to keep them away from the presentation tables.

For noise, we will group the noisier engineering projects near the courtyard and keep the reading and communication projects in the quiet corner. We will not use the gym's main sound system for announcements; instead we will use a small PA at the entrance. Presentations will be scheduled in fifteen minute blocks with five minute transitions. Judges will rotate in pairs, and each pair will see no more than twelve projects.

The schedule is tight. Eight in the morning, student setup. Nine, welcome and safety briefing. Nine thirty to eleven thirty, judging. Eleven thirty to twelve thirty, lunch and family open house. Twelve thirty to two, public viewing and hands on demos. Two to two thirty, keynote. Two thirty to three, awards and cleanup. We need everyone out by four because the gym has an evening event.

The budget needs a decision. Venue is free under Plan A. Tables and chairs are already in the school, so zero. Printing is seven hundred because we have more projects and need floor maps. Awards are five hundred. Snacks are four hundred. AV rental is seven hundred. Speaker is five hundred. Safety supplies, tape, signage, and extension cords are three hundred. That totals three thousand one hundred, leaving nine hundred for contingency. The PTA can cover it, but we need receipts within two weeks.

Volunteers are the biggest risk. We need twelve judges, four check in volunteers, two first aid volunteers, two floaters, and one AV lead. So far we have six judges and two check in volunteers. The college outreach coordinator can send five students, but they need service hours forms. We will ask the high school science club for the rest. If we do not have twelve judges by two weeks before the event, we will switch to a poster walk instead of individual judging.

Owners and deadlines. Ms. Chen owns the venue request and the safety floor plan, due four weeks before. Mr. Patel owns the budget and receipts, due three weeks before. The PTA liaison owns the snack order and volunteer signup, due two weeks before. The AV club owns the PA and livestream test, due one week before. I will own the project registration form and the judge assignment. The registration form opens next Monday and closes three weeks before the fair. Every project needs a teacher sponsor and a safety form at registration, not the day of.

We also need a communication plan. One email to families this week with the date and the registration link. One email to teachers next week with the safety rules. A reminder two weeks before with the drop off map. A final email the day before with parking and lunch information. The school newsletter gets a short paragraph and a photo from last year.

Let's make the decision. We choose Plan A, the gym plus courtyard, with the quiet corner and the smaller PA. We cap registration at one hundred and twenty projects. We keep the college atrium as a backup only if the gym becomes unavailable. We approve the three thousand one hundred dollar budget and hold nine hundred as contingency. We confirm the schedule and the owners. If the judge count is below ten two weeks before, we switch to poster walk and reduce the judging block by thirty minutes.

One final point: accessibility. The gym entrance has a ramp, but the courtyard has a small step. We will put a temporary ramp at the courtyard door and reserve the front row for wheelchairs and strollers. The registration form will ask about accessibility needs so we can plan ahead. Good. That is consensus. I will send the minutes tonight and create the shared checklist. Thank you.
