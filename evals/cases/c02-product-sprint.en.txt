Let's start the sprint planning meeting. We have a five person product team and two weeks. The goal for this sprint is to ship the new onboarding flow to a small group of beta users, not to finish everything on the backlog. Please keep that goal in mind when we discuss scope. I want us to leave with a committed set of stories, one owner for each, the dependencies, and the risks we accept.

First, the business context. Last month only thirty eight percent of new users completed onboarding. The biggest drop off is at the workspace setup step, where people do not understand whether they need to invite their team right away. Support gets about twenty tickets a week about that screen. The design team has three concepts: a shorter single page flow, a guided checklist that appears after signup, and an import workspace option for users who already have data. We cannot build all three in one sprint.

The engineering capacity is sixteen developer days. Alex is on backend, Priya and Sam are on frontend, Diego is on infrastructure, and Mei is our designer who can pair on implementation but is only available four days. We also have one QA contractor for the second week. There is a company holiday next Friday, so the first week is four days and the second week is five, but QA cannot start before staging is stable.

Here are the candidate stories. Story one, shorten the workspace setup page from four steps to two and remove the mandatory invite. That is about three frontend days and one backend day. Story two, add a progress checklist after signup. That is four frontend days, plus analytics, plus two backend days if we want to remember checklist state. Story three, import an existing workspace from a CSV. That is six backend days and two frontend days, and it depends on the new data model that Diego is still migrating. Story four, capture onboarding events and build a dashboard. That is two days from Diego and half a day from Alex. Story five, fix the invitation email bug. That is one day and it is already causing support load.

We also have two unplanned commitments. Legal wants an updated consent screen by the end of week one because of a new privacy requirement. That is one day of frontend and half a day of review. And the mobile team needs a small API field for their release, which is half a day on backend. These are not negotiable this sprint, so put them in the capacity budget before we choose stories.

The design team recommends story one first. It directly addresses the drop off, it does not depend on the migration, and it is small enough to finish in the first week. Story two is valuable, but if we add it now we will not have time to test both. Story three is strategically important, but the data model will not be ready until the end of week one, so it is too risky for this sprint. Story four is necessary because we cannot tell whether the change worked, but we can limit it to three events instead of a full dashboard. Story five is a quick win and should be included.

Now the dependencies. Story two depends on the checklist API, which does not exist. Story three depends on the migration ticket, owned by Diego, and that migration is currently blocked by two legacy tables. The consent screen depends on legal review, which has not been scheduled. The invitation email bug has a suspected race condition and needs a reproduction from support. We should not start anything whose dependency is unknown by the end of day two.

The risks are also clear. If we commit to story one and story two, QA only has three days and will test both flows, which is not enough. If we drop story two, we lose the guided experience the design team believes will improve activation. If we add story three after the migration, we may not finish it and we will have a half built import screen. If we ignore analytics, we will ship blind. We need to choose a scope that is small enough to finish and measurable.

I propose the following sprint goal: ship the shortened onboarding flow to beta, fix the invitation bug, capture three onboarding events, and prepare the import story for the next sprint. We do not build the checklist this sprint. We keep the checklist design ready and put it at the top of the next sprint backlog. For the import, we write the technical design and create the migration dependency, but we do not start the UI. This gives us a complete, shippable slice and a clear next step.

Let's break it down. Story one is owned by Priya, with Sam reviewing the frontend and Alex handling the backend flag. Story five is owned by Alex and Sam; they need a reproduction from support by Tuesday. Story four is owned by Diego; we track only three events, signup started, workspace created, and invite sent, and the dashboard query is a saved SQL view, not a new UI. The consent screen is owned by Mei and Priya, with legal review on Wednesday. The mobile API field is owned by Alex and is due Thursday.

Now the timeline. Week one: story one implementation Monday through Wednesday, consent screen Wednesday, invitation bug reproduction Tuesday and fix Thursday, analytics events Wednesday and Thursday. Week two: staging stabilization Monday, QA Tuesday and Wednesday, beta rollout Thursday, buffer Friday. The mobile API field should ship Tuesday so it does not collide with QA. The import design happens Thursday of week one and does not block anything.

The risk we accept is that the checklist is delayed by one sprint. The mitigation is that the shorter flow may already recover enough activation. The risk we do not accept is shipping both flow changes untested. If QA finds a blocker on Tuesday of week two, we delay beta by one week rather than ship a broken onboarding. If the invitation bug reproduction cannot be found by Wednesday, we keep story five in the sprint but downgrade it to an investigation, and we do not claim it as shipped.

Any objections? Hearing none, we have consensus on the sprint goal, the scope, the owners, the dependencies and the accepted risk. I will post this summary in the sprint channel today.
