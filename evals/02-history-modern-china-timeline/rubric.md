# Factual answer

An accurate timeline should include:

- Opium War: 1840–1842; Qing defeat and the Treaty of Nanjing, with Hong Kong ceded in 1842; the first of the unequal-treaty period.
- The Self-Strengthening Movement: 1861–1895.
- First Sino-Japanese War: 1894–1895; Taiwan ceded under the Treaty of Shimonoseki in 1895.
- Hundred Days' Reform: 1898.
- The 1911 Revolution: October 1911–February 1912; the Qing monarchy ended and the Republic of China was established.
- Border and maritime changes should distinguish the opening of treaty ports/coastal concessions from the loss of border land or sovereignty. The 1858 Treaty of Aigun established a border along the Amur and left the north-bank territory to Russia; the 1860 Convention of Beijing ceded the territory east of the Ussuri to Russia.

# Scoring: 10 points

- 2 points: correct chronological order from 1840–1842 through 1911–1912.
- 2 points: shows the Opium War and 1842 opening/ceding context at the correct point on the timeline.
- 2 points: accurately marks the Self-Strengthening Movement and the Hundred Days' Reform as distinct stages.
- 1 point: shows a historically accurate maritime territorial change, such as Hong Kong in 1842 or Taiwan in 1895, or a coastal treaty-port opening.
- 2 points: explicitly includes one northeastern border territorial change: 1858 Aigun with territory north of the Amur or 1860 Beijing with territory east of the Ussuri shown as transferring to Russia.
- 1 point: marks the 1911 Revolution and the end of imperial rule at the correct endpoint.

# Accepted equivalents

- Treaty of Nanjing may be shown as a port-opening/cession symbol, and 1842 may be attached to Hong Kong's cession.
- Shanghai, Guangzhou, or another treaty port may represent coastal opening; the exact port name is accepted if its opening belongs to the period.
- Taiwan may be represented on a map inset or by an eastward cession arrow.
- “洋务运动” may be labeled in Chinese or shown with arsenals, railways, shipyards, and the 1861–1895 range.
- A Qing/Republic transition symbol is accepted for 1911; it need not show the specific Wuchang uprising if the 1911 revolution is otherwise clear.
