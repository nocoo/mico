# Factual answer

For angles in the first quadrant:

- 30°: sin = 1/2, cos = √3/2, tan = 1/√3 = √3/3.
- 45°: sin = √2/2, cos = √2/2, tan = 1.
- 60°: sin = √3/2, cos = 1/2, tan = √3.
- On the unit circle, the point is (cos θ, sin θ). The 30°, 45°, and 60° terminal points are (√3/2, 1/2), (√2/2, √2/2), and (1/2, √3/2).
- Tangent on the standard unit-circle tangent line x = 1 is the signed y-coordinate of the intersection of the terminal ray with x = 1.
- As θ increases from 30° to 60° in the first quadrant, sin and tan increase while cos decreases.

# Scoring: 10 points

- 2 points: unit circle is centered at the origin with radius 1 and x/y axes clearly labeled.
- 2 points: all three terminal rays and points are placed in approximately correct first-quadrant positions.
- 2 points: exact coordinate pairs are correct for 30°, 45°, and 60°.
- 2 points: sin/cos/tan exact values are all correct for all three angles, with no decimal-only requirement.
- 1 point: a correct geometric construction of the tangent is identifiable, such as a segment on x = 1 or the tangent at the terminal point.
- 1 point: trend table or arrow sequence correctly shows sin/tan increasing and cos decreasing from 30° to 60°.

# Accepted equivalents

- Decimal approximations may supplement exact forms but cannot replace the exact values.
- A table, vertical list, or labeled reference triangles are accepted for the exact values.
- The tangent intersection may be drawn outside the unit circle along x = 1, or represented by a vertical tangent segment.
- A 30°–60°–90° triangle and a 45°–45°–90° triangle are both accepted supporting figures.
