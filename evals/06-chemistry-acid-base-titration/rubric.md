# Factual answer

For 25.0 mL of 0.100 mol/L HCl titrated with 0.100 mol/L NaOH:

- Reaction: H⁺ + OH⁻ → H₂O, or HCl + NaOH → NaCl + H₂O.
- Initial pH of 0.100 mol/L HCl is 1.00 at 0 mL NaOH.
- Equivalence volume is 25.0 mL NaOH: n(HCl) = n(NaOH) = 0.00250 mol.
- At equivalence, pH is approximately 7.00 for a strong acid–strong base titration at 25°C.
- Before equivalence, excess H⁺ remains; after equivalence, excess OH⁻ remains.
- A common excess point is 50.0 mL NaOH: 0.00250 mol OH⁻ is in excess in 75.0 mL total volume, giving [OH⁻] ≈ 0.0333 mol/L and pH ≈ 12.52.

# Scoring: 10 points

- 2 points: curve has pH on the vertical axis and NaOH volume on the horizontal axis, with a sharp rise near 25.0 mL.
- 2 points: initial point is at 0 mL and pH approximately 1.00, not pH 7 or an already weak-acid curve.
- 2 points: equivalence is exactly near 25.0 mL and pH approximately 7.00.
- 2 points: before-equivalence region is identified by excess H⁺/HCl, and after-equivalence region by excess OH⁻/NaOH.
- 1 point: shows or labels NaCl/water as the principal neutral-salt product at equivalence.
- 1 point: includes a representative post-equivalence rise and optional pH 12.5 at 50.0 mL without creating a second equivalence point.

# Accepted equivalents

- Equivalence may be shown with a dashed 25.0 mL guide and a midpoint on the steep segment.
- Initial pH 1 and equivalence pH 7 may be approximate if clearly assigned to 25°C strong-acid/strong-base conditions.
- A 50.0 mL overshoot point is accepted as a correct example but not required.
- Ionic labels H⁺, Cl⁻, Na⁺, OH⁻, H₂O are accepted individually or as a correctly combined phase description.
