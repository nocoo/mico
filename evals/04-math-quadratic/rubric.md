# Factual answer

For y = -x² + 4x - 3:

- Vertex: (2, 1).
- Axis: x = 2.
- Zeros: x = 1 and x = 3.
- y-intercept: (0, -3).
- The parabola opens downward because the leading coefficient is negative; vertex (2, 1) is a maximum value of 1.
- The vertex-centered rectangle with width 4 extends horizontally from x = 0 to x = 4. It is not necessarily a bounding box for the full parabola but must clearly be labeled as centered at the vertex.

# Scoring: 10 points

- 2 points: correct coordinate axes, scale, and downward-opening parabola.
- 2 points: vertex exactly at (2, 1) and axis clearly shown as x = 2.
- 2 points: both zeros at (1, 0) and (3, 0), and y-intercept at (0, -3).
- 1 point: states or visually distinguishes that the vertex is a maximum and gives y_max = 1.
- 2 points: rectangle is centered at x = 2 and has left/right boundaries x = 0 and x = 4.
- 1 point: labels and visual separation prevent the rectangle from being mistaken for a second parabola or the axis of symmetry.

# Accepted equivalents

- Handwritten exact coordinate marks are accepted instead of a numeric axis tick at every unit.
- The downward-opening curve may pass through the exact four points and need not be perfectly continuous.
- The rectangle may be drawn with dashed edges or a translucent fill.
- “Maximum” may be represented by an upward-to-downward curved arrow, if accompanied by a correct numeric label y = 1.
