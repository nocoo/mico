# Factual answer

- Height loss 5 m for a 2 kg block gives gravitational potential energy mgh = 2 × 10 × 5 = 100 J.
- On a frictionless incline, at the bottom the block has kinetic energy 100 J and speed v = √(2gh) = 10 m/s.
- Equal masses undergo a one-dimensional elastic collision. Conservation of momentum and kinetic energy means the 2 kg first block transfers all velocity and stops, while the second block moves at 10 m/s.
- The second block's kinetic energy is 100 J. If it compresses a spring of k = 100 N/m from the kinetic state and then is momentarily at rest, 1/2 kx² = 100 J, giving x = √(2 × 100/100) = √2 m ≈ 1.414 m.
- During compression, kinetic energy decreases and spring potential energy increases; at maximum compression the second block has zero speed and 100 J is stored in the spring.
- No friction or other dissipative force is included, so the total mechanical energy remains 100 J in each ideal segment.

# Scoring: 10 points

- 3 points: incline stage is quantitatively correct: 100 J gravitational potential at 5 m and 100 J kinetic/10 m/s at the bottom.
- 2 points: elastic collision is shown as 10 m/s → 0 m/s for the first block and 0 → 10 m/s for the second block, with momentum 20 kg·m/s before and after.
- 2 points: spring maximum compression is 1.414 m and the corresponding energy is 100 J spring potential.
- 2 points: energy bar or flow diagram shows conservation and transfer: gravitational → translational kinetic → spring potential, with no unmodeled loss.
- 1 point: layout clearly separates incline, collision, and compression stages and identifies key velocities and stationary states.

# Accepted equivalents

- Horizontal velocity arrows may be labeled +10 m/s and 0 m/s instead of using a coordinate sign.
- Momentum may be displayed as 20 kg·m/s or as 2×10 kg·m/s before and after the collision.
- Compression may be labeled 100 J spring energy and √2 m without a decimal approximation.
- Energy bars may be pies, Sankey-style arrows, or stacked segments, provided their totals remain 100 J at every ideal stage.
- A spring with a short maximum-compression inset is accepted if the final resting position is explicitly temporary or the full cycle is not claimed.
