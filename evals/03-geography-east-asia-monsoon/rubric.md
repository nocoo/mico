# Factual answer

- In summer, warm Eurasian low pressure and comparatively high pressure over the Pacific create onshore southeasterly or southerly monsoon winds; the rain belt advances northward from South China toward the Yangtze–Huaihe and North China regions.
- In winter, cold Siberian–Mongolian high pressure produces offshore northwesterly or northerly winds and generally dry conditions over East Asia.
- South China is warm and rainy, especially in the summer monsoon season.
- North China has a warm, rainy summer and cold, dry winter; precipitation is highly concentrated in summer.
- Northwest China is mainly interior and arid, with low annual precipitation and weak monsoon influence. It may still have a relative warm-season or summer maximum, but that maximum must be lower in total than South or North China.

# Accepted equivalents

- Summer arrows may point southeast-to-northwest or from ocean toward land; wind direction need not be one exact line, but must be onshore.
- Winter arrows may be shown as cold dry continental flow toward the ocean.
- A migration of the rain belt may be shown by June–July–August bands rather than a precise date line.
- Precipitation may be shown as bars, short monthly patterns, or a three-row comparison; exact monthly values are not required.
- A simplified line at 30–40°N may represent the North China region if it is clearly labeled.
