# Factual answer

Li Bai's _Quiet Night Thought_ depicts moonlight before the bed that the speaker mistakes for frost, a raised gaze toward the bright moon, and a lowered gaze in homesickness. A valid visual answer need not include the poem's text.

# Scoring: 10 points

- 2 points: clearly recognizable moonlight scene seen from inside a simple room, with a bed or couch and a window or door framing the moon.
- 2 points: moonlight entering or spreading across the foreground so it plausibly reads as the "bright moonlight before the bed" image.
- 2 points: includes a frost-like pale ground or floor effect without relying on written labels.
- 2 points: depicts both upward moon-gazing and downward homesick reflection, using pose, eye/gaze direction, or a compact before/after visual sequence.
- 1 point: a distant hometown is conveyed by a village, rooftops, road, mountains, or a remembered place, rather than a generic landscape only.
- 1 point: composition is a single illustrated scene, contains no text, numbers, arrows used as labels, or explanatory boxes.

# Accepted equivalents

- A bed, mat, low table, or simplified indoor sill may stand for the speaker's room.
- Frost can be rendered as pale crystalline floor patches, silvered ground, or the moonlight's angular shadows.
- Homesickness may be shown through a downward gaze, a body turn toward the remembered landscape, or a diptych-style pair of panels, provided the result remains pure illustration.
- A distant village may be used instead of mountains; either must visually establish separation from the moonlit room.
